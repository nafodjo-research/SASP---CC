\section{Additional Theoretical and Econometric Details}
\label{app:details}

This appendix provides details on the tournament performance model (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms_performance}), including the control-function approach used for non-Grand-Slam events, and additional tables for the non-Grand-Slam control function analysis.

\subsection{Tournament Trajectory Likelihood}
\label{app:trajectory_likelihood}

Let player $i$ enter tournament $t$ and face a sequence of opponents indexed by match $m = 1,2,\dots$. Define the binary outcome:
\begin{equation}
w_{itm} = \mathbf{1}[\text{player $i$ wins match $m$ at tournament $t$}]
\end{equation}

Define the stopping time:
\begin{equation}
T_{it} = \min \{ m : w_{itm} = 0 \}
\end{equation}
with the convention that if the player wins the tournament, $T_{it} = R_t + 1$, where $R_t$ is the total number of rounds.

The tournament trajectory is:
\begin{equation}
F_{it} = (w_{it1}, w_{it2}, \dots, w_{it,T_{it}})
\end{equation}

Let the match-level win probability be:
\begin{equation}
p_{itm} = P(w_{itm} = 1 \mid X_{itm}, D_{ie}, v_{ie})
\end{equation}

Under conditional independence across matches given covariates:
\begin{equation}
P(F_{it}) =
\left( \prod_{m=1}^{T_{it}-1} p_{itm} \right)
\times (1 - p_{it,T_{it}})
\end{equation}

If the player wins all rounds:
\begin{equation}
P(F_{it}) = \prod_{m=1}^{R_t} p_{itm}
\end{equation}

Taking logs:
\begin{equation}
\log P(F_{it}) =
\sum_{m=1}^{T_{it}} \left[
w_{itm} \log p_{itm} + (1 - w_{itm}) \log(1 - p_{itm})
\right]
\end{equation}

This establishes that the likelihood factorizes exactly into match-level Bernoulli contributions.

\subsection{Connection to an Inhomogeneous Geometric Distribution}
\label{app:geometric}

The stopping time $T_{it}$ follows an inhomogeneous geometric distribution:
\begin{equation}
P(T_{it} = m) =
\left( \prod_{s=1}^{m-1} p_{its} \right) (1 - p_{itm})
\end{equation}

with success probabilities varying across matches. This differs from the standard geometric distribution due to heterogeneity in opponent strength and match context.

The expected number of wins is:
\begin{equation}
E[W_{it}] = \sum_{r=1}^{R_t} P(T_{it} > r)
= \sum_{r=1}^{R_t} \prod_{s=1}^{r} p_{its}
\end{equation}

This identity underlies the simulation-based estimators used in the main text.

\subsection{Match-Level Model and Likelihood Reduction}
\label{app:match_model}

The match-level probability is specified as:
\begin{equation}
p_{itm} = \Lambda(\beta' X_{ijm} + \gamma' Z^{pre}_{ie} + \delta D_{ie} + \rho v_{ie})
\end{equation}
where $X_{ijm}$ contains the match-level pairwise covariates (ranking points difference / 1000 linear and quadratic, Elo difference / 100 linear and quadratic, surface Elo difference / 100 linear and quadratic, Laplace-smoothed H2H win proportion, H2H encounter count, age difference, and clay and grass surface indicators) and $Z^{pre}_{ie}$ contains the pre-treatment focal player characteristics (ranking points / 1000 linear and quadratic, Elo / 100 linear and quadratic, surface Elo / 100 linear and quadratic, and player age).

Substituting into the trajectory likelihood and using the log decomposition yields:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i,t,m}
\left[
w_{itm} \log \Lambda(z_{itm})
+ (1 - w_{itm}) \log (1 - \Lambda(z_{itm}))
\right]
\end{equation}
where $z_{itm} = \beta' X_{ijm} + \gamma' Z^{pre}_{ie} + \delta D_{ie} + \rho v_{ie}$.

Thus, estimation reduces to a standard logit on the stacked match-level data.

\subsection{Control Function Approach}
\label{app:first_stage}

The control function corrects for endogenous LL assignment at non-Grand-Slam events. The selection probability $P_i^{LL}$ is constructed from predicted match-winning probabilities obtained from a weighted logit model estimated on all qualifying round matches (Section~\ref{app:instrument}), combined via exact enumeration of all final-round outcome combinations. Under the maintained assumption that the latent selection error and the outcome error are jointly normal:
\begin{equation}
(u_{ie}, \varepsilon_{itm}) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)
\end{equation}
the generalized residual is computed analytically from $P_i^{LL}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{v}_{ie} = D_{ie} \cdot \frac{\phi(\Phi^{-1}(P_i^{LL}))}{P_i^{LL}} - (1 - D_{ie}) \cdot \frac{\phi(\Phi^{-1}(P_i^{LL}))}{1 - P_i^{LL}}
\end{equation}
where $\phi(\cdot)$ and $\Phi(\cdot)$ are the standard normal PDF and CDF. Including $\hat{v}_{ie}$ in the outcome equation corrects for selection on unobservables. The coefficient $\rho$ on $\hat{v}_{ie}$ tests for endogeneity: if $\rho \neq 0$, LL assignment is correlated with unobserved determinants of the outcome.

\subsection{Selection Probability Construction}
\label{app:instrument}

The selection probability underlying the non-Grand-Slam control function and the tournament performance model is computed from the full combinatorial structure of qualifying match pairings. For each player $i$ at event $e$ with $N_e$ players in the final round of qualifying and $c_e$ LL slots available, the selection probability is:
\[
P_i^{LL} = \Pr\bigl(\text{rank among losers} \leq c_e \mid i \text{ loses}\bigr)
\]
Construction proceeds in two steps. First, a weighted logit model of match outcomes is estimated on qualifying round matches at non-Grand-Slam LL-granting events, separately for each tour (ATP: $N = 9{,}775$; WTA: $N = 10{,}199$ qualifying matches). The match-level covariates $X_{ijm}$ include ranking points difference / 1000 (linear and quadratic), Elo difference / 100 (linear and quadratic), surface Elo difference / 100 (linear and quadratic), Laplace-smoothed head-to-head win proportion, head-to-head encounter count, and age difference, along with event-level surface indicators (clay and grass). The logit is estimated by weighted MLE, with weights equal to the total points in play (sum of service points) in each match; qualifying round number serves as a fallback weight when service point data are unavailable. Estimating on all qualifying rounds---not just the final round---improves parameter precision by exploiting the full set of qualifying matches. The same logit specification is used for the selection probability, the tournament performance model, and the treatment dose analysis. This yields predicted match-winning probabilities $\hat{P}(a_m \to b_m \mid X, Z)$ for each final-round qualifying match $m$ between players $a_m$ and $b_m$; players with missing pre-match rankings are assigned a default predicted win probability of 0.5.

Second, given these predicted probabilities and the $N_e - 1$ other final-round qualifying matches (excluding player $i$'s match, since $i$ is conditioned on having lost), the probability is computed by exact enumeration over all $2^{N_e - 1}$ outcome combinations. Each combination has probability equal to the product of the individual match outcome probabilities from the logit (invoking conditional independence of match outcomes given observables). For each combination, the set of losers is determined and their rankings compared; $P_i^{LL}$ is the sum of probabilities of all combinations in which $i$'s rank falls within the top $c_e$ among losers. When $N_e - 1$ exceeds 20 (making exact enumeration computationally prohibitive), the probability is approximated by Monte Carlo simulation with 10,000 draws; this applies to 115 of 5,208 observations. The resulting $P_i^{LL}$ is systematically lower than a naive Bernoulli convolution that treats qualifying outcomes as independent coin flips without accounting for the match pairing structure (mean 0.279 vs.\ 0.506 across the non-GS sample). The difference arises because exact enumeration correctly conditions on the specific draw structure---which players are paired against each other---rather than treating each player's loss probability as independent of the draw.

\subsection{Simulation-Based Counterfactuals}
\label{app:simulation}

Define counterfactual probabilities:
\begin{equation}
p_{itm}(d) = \Lambda(X_{itm}'\hat{\beta} + \hat{\delta} \cdot d)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Expected wins:}
\begin{equation}
E[W_{it}(d)] = \sum_{r=1}^{R_t} \prod_{s=1}^{r} p_{its}(d)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Tournament win probability:}
\begin{equation}
\pi_{it}(d) = \prod_{r=1}^{R_t} p_{itr}(d)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Average marginal effect:}
\begin{equation}
AME = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i,t,m}
\left[ p_{itm}(1) - p_{itm}(0) \right]
\end{equation}

All quantities are computed for every player-tournament observation in the data and then averaged, integrating over actual draw compositions and opponent strengths.

\subsection{Bootstrap Inference}
\label{app:bootstrap}

Inference for the tournament performance model is conducted via player-level block bootstrap:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Resample players $i$ with replacement.
\item Retain full histories across all events and matches for each sampled player.
\item Re-estimate the outcome equation with a fresh draw of player clusters.
\item Recompute all counterfactual quantities (AME, expected wins, tournament win probability).
\end{enumerate}

This preserves within-player dependence across tournaments and matches. Standard errors and confidence intervals are computed from the bootstrap distribution using 200 replications.

\subsection{Additional Identification Discussion}
\label{app:identification}

Identification of the tournament performance model relies on three conditions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Exogeneity of the withdrawal process conditional on rank ordering.} Main draw withdrawals must be independent of the specific identity of qualifying losers. This is plausible because withdrawals are driven by injury or scheduling among main draw players, who are ranked well above the qualifying losers.
\item \textit{Independence of $P_i^{LL}$ from match-level shocks $\varepsilon_{itm}$.} The selection probability depends on the qualifying match outcomes of other players, which are independent of the focal player's future match-level shocks.
\item \textit{Correct specification of the selection probability.} The exact enumeration correctly models the probability that player $i$'s rank falls within the top $c$ among losers given the draw structure. The joint normality of $(u_{ie}, \varepsilon_{itm})$ is a maintained assumption. Misspecification of either could affect the control function correction and bias $\hat{\delta}$.
\end{itemize}

Potential violations arise if withdrawals correlate with unobserved player-specific shocks (e.g., if a main draw player withdraws because they know they will face a particular qualifying loser). The institutional structure makes this unlikely: main draw withdrawals are typically announced before the draw is made, and the identity of potential replacements is not known to withdrawing players.

\subsection{Non-Grand-Slam Tournament Performance Model}
\label{app:nongs_tournament}

At non-Grand-Slam events, LL assignment is determined by ranking among qualifying losers rather than by lottery, so the treatment indicator $D_{ie}$ is potentially endogenous. I use a control function approach: the generalized residual $\hat{v}_{ie}$, computed analytically from the selection probability $P_i^{LL}$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:first_stage}), is included in the outcome logit to absorb correlation between LL assignment and unobserved player quality. Standard errors are obtained via player-level block bootstrap (200 replications).

The ATP non-GS estimate is $\hat{\delta} = +0.033$ (bootstrap SE $= 0.050$, $p = 0.513$), based on 113,189 matches from 838 unique players. The WTA non-GS estimate is $\hat{\delta} = -0.060$ (bootstrap SE $= 0.032$, $p = 0.061$), based on 80,168 matches from 747 unique players. The ATP estimate is small and statistically insignificant, consistent with no effect of LL entry on match-level performance. The WTA estimate is marginally significant and negative---an unexpected result that may reflect selection into tougher early-round opponents for ranking-based LL recipients. This finding does not alter the overall conclusion that match-level performance effects are generally null.
